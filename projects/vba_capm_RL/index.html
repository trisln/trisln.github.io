Portfolio management (VBA)

survivor bias, calculation of performance indicators and study of HF alpha using CAPM

Overview

This project pursues several complementary objectives.

First, the raw data extracted from the available database are cleaned, structured, and formatted to ensure consistency and usability for empirical analysis.

Second, fund performance is analyzed by distinguishing between surviving and non-surviving hedge funds in order to assess the impact of survivorship bias on performance results.

Third, a CAPM-based regression framework is implemented to evaluate whether fund returns can be fully explained by market exposure and to estimate the alpha generated by each fund. The analysis is then extended to a multiple regression setting in order to improve the explanatory power of the model.

Finally, a qualitative analysis is conducted to investigate whether certain non-quantitative fund characteristics are associated with systematic differences in performance.

We will use VBA coding in order to carry out this analysis.

Step 1 - Data collection and preprocessing

The project starts with the collection of hedge fund data from Morningstar, covering both active and defunct funds across five major strategies:

  • Long/Short Equity

  • Global Macro

  • Multi-Strategy

  • Event Driven

  • Merger Arbitrage

Additional market and factor indices (equity, fixed income, credit, volatility, commodities) are also provided.

At this stage:

  • all return series are converted to numeric format,

  • monthly periodicity is enforced,

  • missing values and discontinuities are identified.

A preliminary harmonization of dates is performed to ensure consistent time series across funds and indices.

The rend sheet (returns) of the file is on the left, and carac (caracteristics) of data is on the right.

Step 2 — Aggregation of hedge fund returns

All hedge fund return series are aggregated into a single synthetic dataset (based on each strategy file):

  • one row per fund,

  • one vector of returns per fund,

  • associated metadata (strategy, dates, number of observations).

  • one sheet allocated to indices dataset for further calculations later

This aggregation enables automated looping across hundreds of funds and avoids manual manipulation.

Step 3 — Computation of performance indicators

For each hedge fund, classical performance and risk indicators are computed using VBA:

  • Annualized mean return

  • Volatility

  • Sharpe ratio

  • Modigliani–Modigliani measure (M²)

  • Certainty Equivalent (risk aversion = 3)

Each fund is characterized by:

  • strategy,

  • start and end dates,

  • number of observations,

  • survival status (active or defunct)

    • We have defined a surviving fund as one that has reported its monthly returns for the last three months (October to December 2013 in our case).
    • The result is on the last column that’s why you don’t see it below.

These results are stored in a structured output table, enabling large-scale analysis.

Here is the sheet created by the macro referecing the different statistical results, The difference in color between 2 names of the fund is the difference of strategy between them. We use later this color difference to separate them.

Step 4 - Statistical analysis of survivorship bias

For each strategy and fund group (all funds, survivors only, non-survivors only), the following statistics are computed:

  • mean, standard deviation, Sharpe, M2, EC

  • for each quantiles (5%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 95%)

This allows a direct comparison of performance metrics under different sampling assumptions.

The results clearly show that:

  • performance is systematically overstated when only survivors are considered,

  • Sharpe ratios and certainty equivalents are particularly affected.

Step 5 - Linear regression by using the CAPM

To assess market dependence and alpha generation, a CAPM regression is estimated for each hedge fund: $
r_t = \alpha + \beta \times r_{m,t} + \varepsilon_t $

Where: $\ r_t $ is the fund return, $\ r_{m,t} $ is the MSCI World return. For each fund, the following are computed:

  • beta,
  • t-statistics,
  • $\ R^2 $,
  • Jensen’s alpha,
  • active risk (CAPM theory : specific risque)

The low$\ R^2 $ values (here the mean is 0.244) confirm weak correlation between hedge funds and traditional markets, their strategy is not based on market’s momentum (we used MSCI World as the reference index).

We computed the beta and other statistics without the help of the Linest function included in Excel due to poor error handling by this function that didn’t accept any specific values (0, 1 or NA for example).

Step 6 - Multifactor regression and risk decomposition

To go beyond the CAPM, a multifactor regression framework is implemented, including factors such as:

  • equity market returns (MSCI World NR USD),
  • long term sovereign bond returns (Citi WGBI 10+ Yr USD)
  • high yield bond returns (Credit Suisse HY USD) We chose them among others indices, because it represented highest flow of capital and covered different of risk level.

Due to numerical limitations of standard Excel tools, the regression is implemented manually using matrix algebra: $\ \beta = (X^\prime X)^{-1}X^\prime Y $

To address multicollinearity inherent to the data:

  • factors are orthogonalized using the Gram–Schmidt procedure,
  • series are centered to ensure proper alpha estimation.

Variance decomposition is then performed to quantify the contribution of each factor.

We see here that the$\ R^2 \$ is not significitally better here and could be due to an increase of variables more than the impact of them.

We saw that in the last column (Groupe), where we added the part of the variance explained by each indice used for the regression, that the sum of those variances were very high, and near 1 often. It could be explained be multiple reasons :

  • Gram-Schmidt create fictive orthogonal betas (necessary for the regression), but the explicative part of them is still linked to each other, it is then more difficult to find specific explanation of the variance for each of them. As we know, the MSCI took a great part of the variance beofre GS orthogonalisation, and give more space after to other indices.
  • A lot of high sum of variances is due to small number of observations, which engenders a great instability in the computation of betas and variances.
  • Presence of outliers : with the period of 2008 and other bubbles there is a huge impact on equity and HY bond values which create a significant number of outliers that impacts the calculation of variances.

All those reasons could explain this high part of the variance explained by this small amount of factor. We need more data to improve our model, especially in crisis time.

Step 7 — Analysis of hedge fund characteristics and demography

Using fund-level characteristics:

  • leverage,

  • fees,

  • lock-up periods,

  • manager tenure,

  • fund lifetime,

the project analyzes structural differences between active and inactive funds.

Graphical analyses highlight:

  • incentive misalignment,

  • excessive leverage,

  • shorter or longer life cycles depending on strategy.
